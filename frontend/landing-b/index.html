Give your agent a leash, not a key.

Built for money that an agent runs, not holds.

Set it once. The leash shortens by itself and only a human can lengthen it.

Permission in ENS

A role bit on <you>.leash.eth. Revocable, readable by anyone.

Renewal is human

Only a World ID proof moves the clock. The agent can’t.

Enforced in the swap

A SwapVM opcode trims every trade to the live cap.

Funds stay yours

Your vault holds the money. The agent holds permission.

01

Agents hold money now. Every way to give it to them is broken.

Full access

One key, one bug, everything gone.

Approve every action

The human is the bottleneck at 3 a.m.

It keeps happening

May 2026 · and March 2025, same wallet

Grok × Bankr: an AI agent’s wallet drained by a post on X

A prompt hidden in Morse code made the agent move about $150–200k of tokens; the same wallet had lost $330k to the same injection path fourteen months earlier. The agent held full authority over the wallet.

With a leash: one trade bounded by the cap, then decay. No human, no renewal.

November 2024

DEXX: a trading bot that kept the keys

About $30M from more than 8,600 wallets. The bot custodied users’ private keys and leaked them; whoever had the key had everything.

With a leash: the bot never holds the key. It holds a role the owner can revoke, and the funds stay in the owner’s vault.

December 2022

3Commas: 100,000 exchange API keys leaked

Around $22M traded away from users’ accounts. An API key is all-or-nothing: whoever has it trades as you, with no ceiling and no clock.

With a leash: the ceiling is on-chain, shrinks by itself, and only a verified human can raise it.

September 2024

Banana Gun: outbound transfers nobody asked for

About $3M from 11 traders, drained while they used the bot. The bot’s authority over the wallet was unbounded, so one bug was enough.

With a leash: the market can take at most the cap per fill, and the agent can only ever close on its own.

Figures as reported by SlowMist, the teams involved and the trade press; links in docs/PITCH.md.

02

On Aqua a strategy is immutable, so someone must run the loop.

There is no “edit position”: dock(), then ship(). Whoever can dock can pull the whole balance.

03

Decay, not expiry. Expiry is a cliff; decay is a slope.

The cap halves every 24 hours since a verified human last showed up, and is cut to zero at 72.
